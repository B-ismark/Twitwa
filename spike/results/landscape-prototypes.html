Twitwa · landscape, option 3

Twitwa sideways

Three layouts for the unlocked app when the window is wider than it is tall. Each one is drawn at 891×411 dp, a Pixel phone turned on its side. Buttons, the Style strip and the crop takeover use the app's real labels and palette tokens from src/theme.js and src/copy.js. Tap the tabs, and tap the chips and buttons inside the phone.

Layout can't beat the screen height. A 1440×3120 screenshot is tall, so the card's height is capped by the window's height whatever the layout does. The three layouts differ only in how they use the spare width beside the card. The numbers under each phone come from the drawn layout itself.

Rail

Today's controls moved into a column on the right. Same parts, same Style toggle, same crop takeover.

Gains

  • Smallest change to App.js: the tools row and the bar render into one column when wide. shell.js's tool table doesn't change.
  • Both orientations use one interaction model, so one set of flows to test on a device.
  • Share stays under the right thumb.

Costs

  • The Style strip only fits because the tools and the actions each shrink to a single row. Keep the stacked portrait bar and the rail overflows; the badge above says by how much.
  • At 90° with 3-button nav, the rail sits right beside the nav buttons, and only the insets keep them apart.

Watch for

  • No safe-area insets in the app today (fixed paddingTop). This layout needs react-native-safe-area-context first.
  • The update banner has no room above the stage. It would go at the top of the rail.

Inspector

The width beside a tall card is empty anyway, so Style stays open in a side panel. Crop is the only tool you open.

Gains

  • No tap to reach Style: padding, corners and background are always one touch away while the card updates beside them.
  • The wide panel makes the chips comfortable, and it scales well to a Fold or tablet.

Costs

  • Two interaction models: Style is a toggle in portrait and always open in landscape. ed.tool === 'style' means nothing when wide, and rotating mid-edit crosses between the two.
  • The narrowest stage of the three, which hurts wide screenshots most.
  • With gesture nav the panel fits with 0–3 dp to spare, and one render overflowed it by 2 dp. Any larger system text size will overflow it. Rail has about 29 dp to spare.

Watch for

  • The panel's surface sits next to the dark stage in both themes. In the light theme that's a white panel beside an ink stage, which looks like two apps side by side.

Islands

The stage takes the whole window, and controls float on it in two clusters: tools under the left thumb, actions under the right.

Gains

  • The biggest stage and the biggest card, because no chrome takes up a column.
  • Uses both thumbs, which is how a phone is held sideways.

Costs

  • Controls sit on the stage ground (#0E1013 in both themes), so they need their own island tokens, and contrast.py must learn those pairs.
  • A wide screenshot runs under the islands, and so do crop handles near the sides. The overlap badge reports it.

Watch for

  • Touch arbitration: an island has to swallow a touch before the stage's pan and crop gesture handlers see it.
  • The most new code of the three, and no gate loads App.js.
